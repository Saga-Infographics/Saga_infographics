As of October 2026, the length you can post depends on how you make the Reel and which account you use. The limit most often reported is **3 minutes** for uploads, a change Instagram announced in January 2025. Some reports from late 2025 describe **20 minutes** for the Reels camera. We could not open Instagram's Help Center to confirm either figure, so the most reliable check is the length option in your own app.

## How the limit has changed

The sources we read support this sequence:

- **Before 2025:** the standard cap was 90 seconds. One guide, [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/how-long-can-instagram-reels-be/), still describes 90 seconds as the limit for most accounts, with 3-minute tests for some.
- **January 2025:** Instagram head Adam Mosseri announced that Reels uploads could be up to three minutes. He said creators had told Instagram that 90 seconds was too short. [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-3-minutes/737766/) covered the change.
- **November 2025:** Coverage of a Reels camera update described recordings of up to 20 minutes. [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/11/24/meta-is-rolling-out-improvements-to-the-instagram-reels-camera/) reported the update, and coverage quoted Instagram describing it as giving creators "more creative freedom and longer storytelling." We could not find the original Instagram post.
- **July 2026:** A Hindi-language [Patrika report](https://www.patrika.com/latest-technology/instagram-latest-update-users-can-now-upload-3-minute-reels-19331106) describes three-minute uploads as new. Its details match the January 2025 change, so we could not confirm a separate 2026 announcement.

## Why the top results disagree

Search for this question and you will find many guides giving different answers:

| Claim | Where you will see it | What it leaves out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90 seconds for most accounts | Kontentino, and an older Social Media Today article | Written before the 2025 changes |
| 3 minutes for uploads, about 90 seconds in the in-app camera | [Lilach Bullock's 2026 article](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reel-maximum-length/) | Says limits differ by how you create the Reel |
| Up to 20 minutes | [Superprofile](https://superprofile.bio/guides/instagram-reel-size) and [Brand Ghost](https://blog.brandghost.ai/posts/how-long-video-post-instagram/), both citing the Help Center | Neither quotes the page, and the camera update may not apply to every upload |

Many guides do not say when they were last checked. Limits also roll out unevenly, so two accounts can see different maximums on the same day. Those two factors explain most of the contradictions.

## Length and reach are separate questions

The limit tells you what you can post. Reach is a different question, and the evidence there is weaker.

- One guide says Instagram's Help Center states that Reels longer than three minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. We could not verify that on Instagram's own site.
- [Inro](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-length) reports Socialinsider data showing views falling off after 180 seconds, with the strongest reach around 45 to 60 seconds. That is third-party data, not an Instagram statement.
- [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-reel-length-affects-algorithmic-reach-on-instagram/) says Instagram has not published an official breakdown of how length affects distribution.
- [NetInfluencer](https://netinfluencer.com/instagram-rolls-out-views-related-metrics-to-boost-creators-performance) reports that watch time is the main driver of reach and that longer Reels can appear in discovery. That conflicts with a strict three-minute cutoff.

So treat three minutes as a plausible threshold to test on your own account, not a confirmed rule.

## How to check your limit in the app

Because limits vary by account, the app is more reliable than any article, including this one. [Inro](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-length) makes the same point.

1. Open Instagram, tap the create button, and choose **Reel**.
2. Look for the length or duration option in the Reels camera and note the maximum it offers.
3. Go to the upload option and try a clip of three minutes or more, if you have one, to see whether it is accepted.
4. If you run more than one account, repeat the check on each.
5. Write down the date you checked. The limits have changed several times since 2025.

## What this means for editing

Whatever the limit, the opening seconds decide whether people stay. A few habits help:

- **Edit to the idea, not the maximum.** A 40-second Reel with one clear point usually beats three minutes of filler.
- **Plan a reason to keep watching** if you go past 90 seconds, such as a clear hook at the start and a payoff near the end.
- **Compare lengths on similar content.** Post two versions of a similar idea at different lengths and check how long people watch in your Insights.
