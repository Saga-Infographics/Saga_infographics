Yes. Nepal is on Google's list of countries where a Business Profile can be added ([Google: supported countries](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6270107?hl=en)). If your address has no street number, or Google cannot find it, you can place the pin by hand in the location settings. Check eligibility first, because some Nepali businesses, including online-only shops, do not qualify.

## Check eligibility first

Google's eligibility guidance says a business must have in-person contact with customers during its stated hours to qualify. It also says online-only businesses, such as an e-commerce shop with a virtual storefront, cannot get a Business Profile ([Google: eligibility and ownership](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036)). If your business sells only through Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp, this is the first thing to settle.

Google's guidelines also say a virtual office or a rented mailing address where you do not operate is not eligible, and P.O. boxes are not acceptable ([Google: guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Service-area businesses without a storefront have separate rules, so read that page before you choose the service-area option.

## Which case are you in?

| Your situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Official address has a street number and Google finds it on the map | Keep the address and check that the pin is on your entrance |
| No street number, or Google cannot find the address | Set the pin by hand (steps below) |
| Address is a landmark, not your building | Pin the building. Use landmark text only where Google's rule allows it |
| Online-only, P.O. box, or virtual office | Likely not eligible for a Business Profile. Check the eligibility page first |

## Set the pin by hand

Google's pin guide gives these steps ([Google: drop a pin](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6279343?hl=en)):

1. Open your Business Profile, select **Edit profile**, then **Location**.
2. Next to **Business location**, select **Edit**, then **Adjust**.
3. Move the map until the pin sits on your business.
4. Select **Done**, then **Save**.

The Google Maps app has a similar path under Edit profile and Business information.

Timing is one place where the sources disagree. Google's help says edits usually take about 10 minutes to review, but can stay pending for up to 30 days ([Google: Business Profile edits](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038311)). Our advice is to wait for a pending edit to finish before you change the same field again, rather than resubmitting.

## Landmarks and the official address

Many addresses in Kathmandu are written as landmarks: a temple, a school, a chowk, a ward. Google's rule is narrower. Its guidelines say cross-streets and landmarks can be used only where the official address does not accurately pinpoint the business, and that the listing should include only information that is part of the official address ([Google: guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). An older, archived version of those guidelines asked for the precise address, so the rules have changed over time. Follow the current page.

In practice, the pin does the job a landmark would do. If the official address does not put the pin on your building, move the pin rather than stuffing landmark text into the address. Include a ward number only if it is part of your official address.

## Google Maps and the alleys

Google's map coverage is uneven in Kathmandu. A 2023 Kathmandu Post article says Google Maps "cannot provide information on Gallis, the alleys," and reports that local apps Galli Maps and Baato are gaining users for that reason ([Kathmandu Post, March 2023](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2023/03/06/nepali-made-location-apps-gain-credence-for-their-greater-accuracy)). That article is more than two years old, so check how your business appears today. If you are on a gali, check Galli Maps and Baato as well as Google, because some customers will use them.

## Two checks before you edit

- **Look for a second listing.** Search for an existing profile for the same business before you change anything. A duplicate is a plausible cause of a wrong pin. This is our inference, not a documented Google cause.
- **Confirm you can manage the profile.** Only edit a profile you own or manage. If the pin has moved and you do not know why, contact Google through the support options in the help pages rather than guessing.
