To write a Reel hook in Nepali, start with the words your viewers would use, not a translated English line. Keep the hook to one clear question or promise in the first two or three seconds, write it in the language your viewers speak most, and keep English words for brands and tools. Then test two or three versions with native speakers before you film.

## Why the usual hook lists miss Nepali viewers

Most hook templates are written in English. The closest alternatives are Hindi and Hinglish guides, and a Hindi sentence does not translate cleanly into Nepali because word order, verbs and everyday slang differ. Even the Nepal-focused guides we found are short on examples. One [Nepal social media guide](https://arjankc.com.np/blog/ultimate-guide-social-media-marketing-nepal/) advises brands to write in the language their buyers use, which it says often means Romanized Nepali mixed with English product terms. We could not open the full page, so treat that as the guide's advice rather than a measured result.

## What the language data does and does not tell you

Two sources give a picture, but neither answers the question of which script to use:

- A [2021 Nepal social media users survey](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/SocialMediaSurvey_Nepal_2021_CMR.pdf) reports that most users (73.4%) use both Nepali and English on social platforms, and nearly 15% use only Nepali. It does not measure Romanized versus Devanagari script.
- A [2026 arXiv paper on Romanized Nepali](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14171) describes it as the de facto language of Nepali messaging and youth discourse. Its own limitations section says it did not evaluate social media text, so it does not measure how common the script is.

So the data supports a mixed approach: plan for both scripts, and let your own comments and replies tell you which one your audience uses.

## Step 1: Write the idea in plain English, then rewrite it

Pick a hook formula first, then translate the meaning, not the words. Hook guides often use fill-in-the-blank formulas, such as "Stop [habit] if you want [result]" or a problem-and-fix line like "Struggling with breakouts? Try this instead." A [list of 100+ Reel hooks from Creatorsjet](https://www.creatorsjet.com/blog/instagram-reel-hooks) gives more examples, though it is a promotional blog.

Here is one example rewritten in both scripts. These lines are our own, not from a source, so have a native speaker check them before you use them:

| Idea | Romanized Nepali | Devanagari Nepali |
|---|---|---|
| Warn about a common mistake | Reel banauda yo galti nagarnuhos! | Reel बनाउँदा यो गल्ती नगर्नुहोस्! |
| Fix a video problem | Video dhamilo dekhinchha? Yo sajilo upaya hernuhos. | Video धमिलो देखिन्छ? यो सजिलो उपाय हेर्नुहोस्। |

## Step 2: Keep the first two or three seconds to one promise

Hook guides tend to agree that the opening decides whether someone keeps watching. A [Nepal video marketing guide](https://bimalrp.com/video-marketing-nepal/) says the first two to three seconds need to stop the scroll; it is a promotional page, so treat that as the guide's claim, not a measured result. Do not put a greeting, a logo or a long intro in front of the promise. Put the on-screen text and the first spoken line in the same language, so the viewer reads and hears the same thing.

## Step 3: Keep brand names and tool names in English

Mixed speech is normal for Nepali creators. Automatic captions can struggle with names, brands and mixed-language phrases; our [guide to captions for short videos](/blog/how-to-add-captions-to-short-form-videos) notes this as our own observation, not a measured result. Write the verbs and the explanation in Nepali, and keep product names, app names and price terms in English so they are easy to read and easy to correct.

## Step 4: Test before you film

You do not need a large study to catch obvious problems. Write three hooks for the same video: one English, one Romanized Nepali and one Devanagari. Show them to three to five people who match your target viewer, and ask which one they would stop for and what the line means in their own words. If they paraphrase the line differently from how you meant it, rewrite it.

Once you post, compare the first-three-second drop-off across versions if your analytics show it. Instagram's trial Reels feature can also be used to test a version with non-followers first; our [explainer on how trial Reels work](/blog/instagram-trial-reels-how-they-work-2026) covers the rules and where sources disagree.

## A simple script layout

The sample lines in this table are our own, not from a source.

| Part | What it does | Example in Romanized Nepali |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–3 seconds) | One promise or question | Reel banauda yo galti nagarnuhos! |
| Point 1 | The first useful fact | Pahila, frame ko kinara... |
| Point 2 | The second useful fact | Dosro, caption aile pani... |
| Call to action | One next step | Comment ma "script" lekhnuhos. |

Keep the body to two or three points. Every line should be something you can say in one breath.
