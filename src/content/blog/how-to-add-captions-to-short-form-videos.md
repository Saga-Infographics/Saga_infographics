Captions are one of the most searched-for short-form editing questions, and for good reason: many people watch with the sound off, and captions also help viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing or who are watching in a second language. The good news is that all three big platforms can generate captions for you. The catch is that each one handles it differently, and none of them is perfect.

This guide covers the built-in options on Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok, plus when it is worth captioning by hand or in an editor.

## Instagram Reels: the Captions sticker

According to guides from [Kapwing](https://kapwing.com/resources/instagram-reels-captions) and [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/resource/how-to-add-captions-to-reels), the built-in route works like this:

1. Create a new Reel and add your video.
2. Tap **Next** to reach the editor.
3. Open the sticker menu and choose the **Captions** sticker. Instagram transcribes the audio.
4. Pick a caption style (the guides mention options such as classic, typewriter, scroll and fade).
5. Read the transcript and tap words to fix mistakes before you post.

The important limit: the guides say you can edit the generated captions **before** publishing, but not after the Reel is live. So proofread first.

Menu names and positions change over time, and some guides describe the feature slightly differently, so follow the labels you actually see in your app. Language support has also changed since launch. [TechCrunch reported in 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/01/instagram-is-rolling-out-auto-generated-captions-for-videos/amp/) that auto-captions launched in 17 languages, but we could not confirm the current list, so check the app.

## YouTube Shorts: captions arrive after you publish

YouTube Shorts works the other way around. Guides such as [Kapwing's](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-auto-caption-youtube-shorts/) and [Riverside's](https://riverside.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-to-youtube-shorts) say automatic captions are generated after the Short is published, not during upload. Sources disagree on the wait: one says anywhere from a few hours to a few days, another says a minute or two for new videos. Plan for a delay rather than assuming it will be instant.

To correct errors, the guides describe this path:

1. Open YouTube Studio and go to **Content**, then the Shorts list.
2. Select the Short and open **Details**.
3. Use the **Subtitles** tool to edit the text and save.

Kapwing also notes that you can upload your own subtitle file (SRT or SBV) if you already have a transcript.

## TikTok: captions in the editing menu

On TikTok, [SpeakWrite's guide](https://speakwrite.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-on-tiktok/) and [AI Video Cut](https://www.aivideocut.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-to-tiktok-videos) describe adding captions inside the app after you record or upload. You open the editing menu, choose **Captions**, and TikTok generates them from the audio. You can tap a caption to correct it and drag the block to reposition it. The same sources say clear audio improves accuracy, that fast speech and accents can trip the system up, and that you generally cannot edit captions after posting.

## Built-in captions vs. an editor

| Approach | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built-in platform captions | Fast, free, no extra app | Transcription errors, limited styling, often no edits after posting |
| Auto-captions in an editor (for example CapCut's desktop **Auto captions** under Text) | More control over font, colour and timing | Extra export step; the tool vendors writing these guides have a stake in recommending themselves |
| Manual captions | Short videos, names and slang the AI gets wrong | Slow on longer videos |

The practical middle ground many editors use is to auto-generate, then proofread line by line. Automatic transcription tends to stumble on names, brand terms and mixed-language speech, which is common for creators in Nepal who switch between Nepali and English. That last point is our own observation rather than something the sources measured.

## A quick caption checklist

- **Proofread before you post.** Since edits are often locked afterwards, this is your only clean chance.
- **Keep lines short.** Two short lines read faster than a paragraph.
- **Avoid the platform buttons.** Keep captions away from the edges where interface icons sit; check your preview in the app.
- **Match the audio.** Captions should land when the words are spoken, not a beat later.
- **Do not rely on captions alone.** Hook, pacing and clear audio still do most of the work.
