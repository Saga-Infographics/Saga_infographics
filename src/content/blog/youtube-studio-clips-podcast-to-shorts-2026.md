YouTube has extended its Studio clips tool to Shorts. You can cut a segment of a long video, such as a podcast episode, and reframe it as a vertical Short. As of November 2026, the change was announced in a YouTube Help Community post aimed at podcast creators, as [Search Engine Journal reports](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-studio-clips-podcast-episodes-shorts/597408). The coverage agrees on the core feature, but the details vary, and one official help page contradicts the announcement. This post separates what is reported from what you should check on your own channel.

## What the announcement covers

YouTube previewed a clip-to-Shorts feature for podcasters in September 2025, as [TechCrunch reported at the time](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/youtube-to-use-ai-to-help-podcasters-promote-themselves-with-clips-and-shorts/). The November 2026 change is the version that reached creators. Reports say a widescreen episode is reformatted for vertical viewing, with the most important parts of the frame kept in view.

## How to make the clip

According to YouTube's [Video Clips and Shorts help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15824265), you go to **Content**, then **Videos**, open the video, and choose **Clips and Shorts** in the left menu. From there the workflow looks like this:

1. Open the episode and pick the segment, using the transcript or the timeline editor.
2. Let the tool reframe the 16:9 video into a vertical frame.
3. Check the captions, which reports say are added automatically where available.
4. Publish the clip as a Short.

The table below separates what the help page says from what press reports add.

## Where the sources disagree

The Finnish-language version of the same help page says the tool can currently create only 16:9 videos. That contradicts the Shorts announcement. Either the page is out of date or the feature is rolling out unevenly, and our sources do not say which. Before you plan a schedule around it, check whether your channel shows the Clips and Shorts option for a long video at all.

One report also notes that the announcement seems to assume a filmed show and says nothing about podcasts that reach platforms through an RSS feed. If your show is distributed that way, check the upload path yourself.

## A checklist before you publish a clip

These steps are our method, not a rule from YouTube.

1. **Start from the full video on your channel.** The Studio tool works on videos you have uploaded there.
2. **Choose self-contained moments.** Vendor guides in the results suggest clips of about 30 to 60 seconds, found through the transcript. Treat that as a starting point. Shorts can run up to 3 minutes, so see [how long a YouTube Short should be](/blog/how-long-should-youtube-shorts-be).
3. **Watch the whole clip in the preview.** An automatic reframe can cut off a speaker's hands or a second guest in a two-person shot. Fix that before you publish.
4. **Check captions line by line.** If the episode is in Nepali or mixes Nepali and English, auto-captions may not be reliable. Our [guide to Nepali subtitles](/blog/how-to-add-nepali-subtitles-reels-shorts) covers the checks.
5. **Keep the full episode in the podcast playlist** and let the clips sit in the Videos and Shorts tabs, as YouTube's guidance describes.
6. **Run a small test.** Compare a few weeks of tool-made clips with clips you cut by hand, using the same views and retention figures for each.

## What we could not confirm

We could not open YouTube's help pages, Search Engine Journal, ppc.land or eMarketer from this session, so the steps and limits above come from search excerpts and press summaries. An eMarketer summary also says reusing your own episodes will not be penalised, and that Shorts revenue requires 10 million qualified views over a rolling 90 days from February 2027. We left both out because we could not check them against YouTube's own rules. Read YouTube's current Shorts and monetisation pages before you rely on either claim.
