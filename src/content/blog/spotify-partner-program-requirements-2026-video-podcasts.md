As of November 2026, there is no single set of Spotify Partner Program requirements for video podcasts that the sources agree on. Spotify lowered its thresholds in January 2026 and expanded the program to more markets in the autumn, but news reports, consultant blogs and Spotify's own pages give different numbers and different country lists. Your Spotify for Creators dashboard is the only place that reflects your own account, so start there, then use the public sources to understand what is changing.

## What changed in January 2026

Spotify announced a broader set of eligibility thresholds on January 7, 2026, under the headline ["Get engagement, get paid"](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/spotify-partner-program-updates). [TechCrunch's coverage](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/spotify-lowers-monetization-threshold-for-video-podcasts) described the change as a lower bar for video podcasters. We could only read excerpts of the announcement, so the figures below come from news summaries.

The older rules, as TechCrunch described them, were 12 published episodes, 10,000 consumption hours in the previous 30 days, and at least 2,000 people streaming. Later reports describe the new bar as three published episodes, 2,000 consumption hours and 1,000 engaged listeners over the previous 30 days. Spotify also says the program uses streams, not plays, to decide eligibility and revenue, so a video view on YouTube or Instagram does not count toward these numbers.

## Where the sources disagree

The table shows the numbers from each source. None of them is a Spotify page we could read in full.

| Source | Published episodes | Consumption hours | Listeners | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Older rules (TechCrunch coverage) | 12 | 10,000 in prior 30 days | 2,000 people | Before Jan 2026 |
| Later reports of the January change | 3 | 2,000 in prior 30 days | 1,000 engaged listeners | Jan 2026 |
| A consultant's blog | 75 (down from 123) | Not given | Not given | "As of October 2026" |

The consultant's figure does not match the January reports, and we could not check it against Spotify, so treat it with caution. The gap in the table is the main point: a podcaster who reads one article can come away with a very different threshold from the next one.

Country lists also differ. A Spotify support page in search results says the program and Premium video revenue start on October 20, 2026 in Andorra, Italy, Malta, Poland, San Marino and Spain. Other support pages list the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia. Spotify's September 2026 newsroom post about more than 35 new markets names Italy, Spain, Brazil, Mexico and Colombia as examples. [RouteNote's coverage](https://routenote.com/radar/spotify-partner-program-expands-to-more-than-35-new-countries-how-creators-can-make-money/) also reports the expansion. None of the sources we could read names Nepal.

## How to check your own eligibility

1. **Open the Monetize tab in Spotify for Creators.** Spotify's support pages say you can check whether your show meets the criteria there. This is more reliable than any blog, because it reflects your own numbers.
2. **Confirm where the show is hosted.** A [vendor guide to Spotify video podcasts](https://vidpros.com/spotify-video-podcast/) says the show must be hosted on Spotify for Creators to apply. Treat that as a vendor claim until you see it in Spotify's own terms.
3. **Check your country.** Spotify's [Partner Program Terms](https://www.spotify.com/legal/spotify-partner-program-terms/) say you can earn only if your legal address is in a country where the program is available, and Spotify can change that list. If Nepal is not on the list you see in your dashboard, the program is not open to you yet, whatever the blog posts say.
4. **Track consumption hours and listeners, not video views.** Use Spotify's audience numbers for the 30-day window. A rise in YouTube views will not move the Spotify figures.
5. **Do not pause for long.** A support page says that pausing the program for more than six consecutive months can cause a show to lose eligibility.
6. **Plan for changes.** Spotify says the criteria can change at any time, so check the Monetize tab again before you plan around a payout.

## Production point: ad breaks

The same support page says a video episode needs at least one ad break to qualify for Premium video revenue. That affects the edit, not just the upload. If you publish video episodes, plan the ad-break points before you export, and keep the placement consistent across episodes so the show does not depend on a one-off choice. We could not read Spotify's full wording on ad-break length or placement, so check the current guidance before you commit to a format.

## What we could not verify

- Spotify's support and newsroom pages could not be opened in full from our research environment, so the figures come from search excerpts and news summaries.
- The three-episode, 2,000-hour and 1,000-listener figures appear in later reports, but we did not find them on a Spotify page we could read.
- We found no source that says whether Nepal is eligible. We are not making a claim either way.
