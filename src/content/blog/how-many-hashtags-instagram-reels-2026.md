As of November 2026, the most recent reports say Instagram lets you use **up to five hashtags** on a post or Reel. The cap was reported to start rolling out in December 2025. Many guides still give older numbers. We could not open Instagram's Help Center or Meta's newsroom from this session, so the most reliable check is your own caption box.

## What was reported in December 2025

Two news outlets covered a hashtag limit in the same week. [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/12/19/instagram-puts-a-limit-on-hashtag-usage/) ran "Instagram puts a limit on hashtag usage," and [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19) headlined the change as limiting hashtag use to five per Reel and post. [Later's guide](https://later.com/blog/instagram-hashtags/amp) says the five-hashtag cap began rolling out in December 2025 and calls it a platform-enforced limit, not a best practice.

Treat these as one claim, not three confirmations. Most of the other pages we found repeat the same figure, and we could not open the two news articles directly, so we read them only through search excerpts.

## Why the top results disagree

Search for this question and you will find guides with different numbers:

Earlier, [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-testing-new-limits-on-how-many-hashtags-you-can-add-to-your-pos/707471/) reported tests that limited some users to three hashtags. That was a test, not a confirmed rule, and we could not confirm its date.

## Does the limit cover Reels?

Sendible says the limit applies to any content type, including Reels. Most sources that give a number describe the same five-tag ceiling for Reels, so this is the claim with the most agreement. It still rests on secondary reporting. We could not confirm whether hashtags placed in a first comment count toward the same limit, so do not assume they do or do not.

## Do hashtags still help?

Guides disagree on whether hashtags still feed discovery, and we found no Instagram statement that settles it. Sendible advises avoiding broad tags such as #Reels and #Explore, and says Instagram has warned they can hurt performance. We could not check that warning on Instagram's own site.

## How to check the limit yourself

1. Open a new caption in the Instagram app and paste six different hashtags. Note whether the field stops you or shows a message.
2. If the app lets you publish six, use your own account or a test post you can delete to see how many tags appear on the live post.
3. Check the Instagram Help Center and Instagram's Creators account for any update. Write down the date you checked.
4. Repeat the check on each account you run, since rollouts can vary by account.

## Pick tags that fit the Reel

Whatever the limit, the tags you use matter more than the count. [ManyChat's guide](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-hashtags/) suggests cutting any tag you cannot explain for that specific post. [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=727877) suggests roughly two broad tags and three niche tags within a five-tag set, which is a reasonable starting point if you cannot find a rule you trust.

Here is a simple example. Say your Reel is a 30-second beginner dance tutorial. A long list might include #Reels, #Viral, #Dance, #Fyp and #Trending, plus several more. Those are broad and do not describe the video. A five-tag set that fits the Reel could be #DanceTutorial, #BeginnerDance, #LearnToDance, #DanceReels and #Choreography. Each one says something specific about what viewers will see. Use the set on a few posts and compare the results in your own insights before you settle on a list.

## What to do now

Until Instagram publishes its own rule, plan for five. If you have a longer list, cut it to the most specific tags first. Hashtags are only one input to a Reel's reach. The opening seconds and the edit are the parts you control most directly.
