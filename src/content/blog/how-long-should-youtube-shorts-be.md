If you edit or publish YouTube Shorts, you have probably seen two different answers to the same question: 60 seconds or 3 minutes? Both numbers are in circulation, so here is what applies today and how to choose a length that actually helps your videos.

## The maximum length is 3 minutes

YouTube raised the Shorts limit from 60 seconds to **3 minutes** in October 2024. Several guides, including [Async](https://async.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be/) and [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/), report that videos uploaded on or after 15 October 2024 count as Shorts if they meet two conditions:

- The video is **3 minutes or shorter**.
- The video is **vertical or square**.

If a video fails either condition, YouTube treats it as a regular video. That means a 2-minute horizontal clip is not a Short, but a 2-minute vertical one is.

## What about the 60-second limit?

The older 60-second figure still shows up in two places:

- **The in-app Shorts camera.** Guides report that a single recording session in the Shorts camera is capped at 60 seconds. If you want a longer Short, record and edit it elsewhere, then upload it.
- **Older articles.** Many pages were written before the change and were never updated.

Some sources also say that Shorts using YouTube's licensed music clips are still limited to 60 seconds. We could not confirm that against YouTube's own help pages, so check the official documentation before you plan a long Short around licensed audio.

## The best length depends on the video

The maximum is not the target. Sources disagree on a single ideal length, which is a sign that it depends on what you are making. As a starting point:

| Type of Short | Typical length to test |
| --- | --- |
| Trend or audio remix, quick joke | 15-25 seconds |
| Tutorial, product demo, explainer | 30-60 seconds |
| Story or deeper breakdown | 60 seconds to 3 minutes, only if retention holds |

Treat these as starting points to test. One source reports that Shorts between 50 and 60 seconds earned far more views than clips under 10 seconds in a study of 5,400 Shorts. That is a single secondary source, so run your own tests instead of copying the number.

## Retention matters more than length

A longer Short only helps if people keep watching. A 45-second video that most viewers finish usually beats a 2-minute video that most viewers abandon in the first few seconds. Guides on short-form editing keep returning to the same advice:

- **Open with the payoff or a strong hook.** The first one to three seconds decide whether people stay. [Splice](https://spliceapp.com/blog/mastering-short-form-video-editing-guide/) and [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/hook-first-second-video-retention.html) both stress this.
- **Cut dead air.** Jump cuts remove filler and keep the pace up. Aim for roughly 1-3 seconds per shot.
- **Add captions.** Many people watch with the sound off.
- **Keep effects simple.** Too many transitions and effects distract from the message.

## How to choose your length

1. **Write the shortest version first.** Cut everything that does not support the one idea of the video.
2. **Only extend if you have more to say.** Add length when the extra seconds add value, not to fill time.
3. **Check your retention graph.** In YouTube Studio, look at where viewers drop off. If most leave at 20 seconds, a 60-second video is too long for that idea.
4. **Test two lengths.** Publish a short and a long cut of similar topics and compare average view duration.
