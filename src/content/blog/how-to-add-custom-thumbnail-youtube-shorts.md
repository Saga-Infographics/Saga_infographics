You can now upload a custom thumbnail for a YouTube Short, but only if your channel has the option. YouTube began rolling out the feature in late July 2026, starting with Partner Program channels, and uploads are desktop-only according to the coverage we read. If you do not see the option, the older method still works: pick a frame from the video as the cover.

This post is current as of November 2026. It is based on press and guide coverage, not YouTube's own help pages, which we could not open for this post. The sources also disagree on a few details, and those are flagged below.

## What changed in July 2026

Coverage of the announcement says YouTube made custom thumbnails for Shorts available in late July 2026. [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails-2026) dates the announcement to July 24, 2026, while [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/) says the rollout began on July 25. Treat the exact day as one or two days either way.

Three limits appear consistently in the coverage:

- **Eligibility:** access starts with YouTube Partner Program channels. The coverage we read gives no timeline or country list for wider access.
- **Platform:** uploads are desktop-only. Mobile users still choose a frame from the video.
- **Testing:** A/B testing is not included. Long-form videos can run multi-variant thumbnail tests, but Shorts cannot yet, according to [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/social-media-marketing/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails-no-ab-testing/) and [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/).

This is a reversal. In July 2024, YouTube said custom thumbnails for Shorts were not planned for that year ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/)). Some older guides still say uploads are not possible, such as [this fix page](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/cant-change-shorts-thumbnail/). If your Studio shows the upload option, those pages are out of date for your account.

## Check whether you have the option

Open YouTube Studio in a desktop browser, go to Content, and click the edit (pencil) icon on the Short. A [creator news roundup](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/youtube-studio-adds-custom-shorts-thumbnails-and-ai-thumbnail-generation) describes the new thumbnail option in Studio. If there is no upload option, your channel has probably not reached the rollout yet. Check again later rather than building your process around it.

One [vidIQ guide](https://blog.vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails/) claims desktop uploads reached most accounts during 2025. That conflicts with the July 2026 coverage, and we found no source that settles it, so treat the 2025 claim as unconfirmed.

## Uploading on desktop

1. Open YouTube Studio on a desktop browser and select the Short from Content.
2. Click the edit icon, then find the thumbnail section.
3. Choose the upload option and pick your image.
4. Check the preview, then save.

Because mobile cannot upload a custom image in this rollout, design the thumbnail on your computer or export it there before you start the upload.

## Image size and crop

The sources disagree on how the thumbnail is cropped, so design with the safest assumption:

| Setting | What the sources say | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | 1080 × 1920 px, 9:16 vertical ([Moda](https://moda.app/resources/sizes/youtube)) | Most cited figure |
| Usable centre | The middle of the frame, about a 4:5 region ([Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/youtube-shorts-dimensions/)) | One source family, not verified against YouTube |
| Display ratio | Thumbnails show at about 3:2, not 9:16 ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/social-media-marketing/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails-no-ab-testing/)) | Single source |

The practical takeaway is to keep the subject and any text in the centre of the canvas and away from the edges. Some guides also warn against uploading a standard 16:9 image, which gets cropped in the vertical feed. Check the Studio preview before you save, because the preview is the only check we could confirm.

## The frame method, if you do not have the option

The older workaround still works. Make the cover design in an image editor or a design tool, add it to the start of the video timeline as a few frames, export, and upload. In the thumbnail step, select that frame. One [ThumbMagic guide](https://www.thumbmagic.co/blog/youtube-shorts-thumbnail-guide) describes choosing a frame in the mobile app, and you can change the frame after upload.

This method works with any account, but the frame must come from the video itself. A custom image is not the same as a frame, so the result is limited by what the frame looks like at the moment you pick it.

## Plan the cover while you edit

The cover is easier to get right if you plan it during the edit rather than after. Decide which moment in the cut works as the cover, keep that shot clean and well lit, and export a still if you want to design over it. The hook at the start of a Short often works as a cover, but test the frame in the preview before you publish.
