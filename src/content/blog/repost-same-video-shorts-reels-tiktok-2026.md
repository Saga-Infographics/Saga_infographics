You can post the same video to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok, but none of the three treats an identical copy as new work. YouTube's reused-content policy says material repurposed without significant original commentary, modification or value does not qualify for monetization, and permission from the original creator does not change that. Since October 2026, YouTube has also been pushing original Shorts higher in recommendations. The practical answer is to post a version on each platform that adds something new, not the same file everywhere.

## What YouTube's reused-content policy says

YouTube's help page on reused content defines the term as material repurposed from YouTube or elsewhere without significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value ([YouTube Help: reused content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Two points matter if you repurpose:

- **Permission does not settle it.** The policy applies even when you have the original creator's permission, and it is separate from copyright.
- **Commentary and clips can still earn.** Clips, compilations and reactions can be monetized when the creator adds real value. The policy reviews them on that basis, not on whether a clip is borrowed.

## What changed on Shorts in October 2026

Coverage dates YouTube's change to 1 October 2026. YouTube described it as a recommendation update that prioritizes original content and reduces reach for Shorts that re-upload other creators' videos without meaningful additions ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube/youtube-shorts-original-content-reach-update/); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/)). It is not a ban. Coverage quoting a YouTube creator liaison says voice-over narration, minor technical edits and template-based bulk changes do not by themselves make a reused clip original ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/10/youtube-cuts-reach-reuploaded-shorts-originality-push.html)). YouTube has not said how long a channel takes to regain reach after it shifts to original work.

## Instagram and Facebook

- **Facebook (July 2025):** Meta said accounts that repeatedly reuse others' videos can lose monetization access and see reduced distribution. When duplicate videos are detected, the copies get less reach so the original creator keeps the views ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)). Reaction videos and commentary that add a distinct view are still encouraged. Simple reposts, watermarks and clip stitching do not meet the bar.
- **Instagram (April 2026):** Trade coverage says Instagram extended its reduced-reach rules for reposted content from Reels to photos and carousels ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)). The sources disagree on the threshold. One report says an account that posts unoriginal content 10 or more times in 30 days stops getting Reels recommendations. Others describe a test based on whether an account mostly posts others' work. We could not confirm the exact cutoff from Instagram's own pages.

## TikTok

TikTok matters most for money. Its Creator Rewards program depends on originality, and vendor guides say reposts, duets and photo slideshows get low originality scores ([TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/blog/how-tiktok-creator-rewards-work-2026/)). One May 2026 report describes original creators being disqualified with no clear appeal path, but it comes from a single outlet. Check TikTok's own creator pages before you rely on the criteria.

## How to change one shoot for three platforms

1. **Start from your own master file.** Do not repost a download that carries another app's watermark. Sources treat that as unoriginal.
2. **Change the first three seconds for each platform.** A new opening line or on-screen text is an obvious edit, and it is the part viewers see first.
3. **Add something the original does not have.** Your own narration or on-screen commentary on the clip is the clearest case. A new crop or a different caption style alone is not enough.
4. **Check the music.** One vendor guide says a sound licensed in one app may not be available in another, so check each app's library.
5. **Treat clips of other creators as a separate case.** Platform rules and copyright are different questions. Add substantial commentary, and check the licence before you publish someone else's footage.

For example, a 45-minute podcast can yield six clips. Each Short gets its own opening line and a spoken takeaway. Each Reel uses a different cut and caption style. Each TikTok gets its own pacing. This is an illustration, not a result we have measured.
