Instagram Edits' bilingual captions feature translates a Reel's captions into a second language. As of October 2026, the language list reported in coverage does not include Nepali. Hindi, which uses the same Devanagari script as Nepali, is on that list. Because the list comes from secondary reports, check your own app before you plan a caption workflow around it. This matters most for creators whose audience mixes Nepali and English speakers, where a caption in only one language can leave part of the audience reading nothing useful.

## What Instagram added in July 2026

Instagram's Edits app received several updates in 2026. The one that matters for captions arrived in early July. Coverage describes three changes:

- **Bilingual captions:** captions are translated into a second language, as [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/instagram-introduces-bilingual-captions-for-creators-on-edits/) and [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/486573/instagram-edits-bilingual-captions-overlay-templates-clip-lock-seasonal-sound-effects.html/amp) both report.
- **Overlay templates and clip lock:** templates can now carry overlays, and you can lock selected clips so they stay in place when a template is reused. FoneArena reports this too.
- **Seasonal sound effects:** new effects were added, including one called "summer energy," according to [CSR Journal](https://thecsrjournal.in/instagram-introduces-bilingual-captions-creative-tools-latest-update/).

The reports do not agree on every detail. [Onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/bilingual-captions-reels-reichweite-neue-zielgruppen) dates its coverage 2 July 2026, while MediaBrief gives 5 July. [The Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/05/07/2026/instagram-launches-update-for-video-editing-app-edits) describes a feature that translates comments and captions into more than 15 languages, which is not quite the caption feature the other outlets describe. We could not open most of these pages from our research environment, so the details above come from search summaries of the articles. Treat the exact wording as unverified.

## Is Nepali on the language list?

MediaBrief's list has 15 languages: English, Indonesian, Russian, Portuguese, Gujarati, Spanish, Hindi, Korean, Bengali, German, Italian, Thai, French, Japanese and Kannada. Nepali is not on it. Hindi is, and so is Bengali, a neighbouring South Asian language written in its own script. It is tempting to assume Nepali will follow, but no source we found says so.

Two explanations fit the evidence. The feature may be limited to the listed languages, or the list may vary by region or app version. The sources we could read do not settle which.

## How to check in your own app

Since we could not confirm the menu path from the coverage, check it directly:

1. Update the Instagram app and open Edits. If the feature is missing, your version or region may not have it yet.
2. Open a clip with captions and look for the translation or caption options. Check whether Nepali appears in the language picker.
3. Test with a short clip, about 15 seconds. Write one version in Devanagari Nepali and one in Roman Nepali, since many people write Nepali in Latin letters.
4. Preview the result on a phone. Confirm that Devanagari characters render correctly and that the text is not hidden behind the on-screen buttons.
5. Read the final preview yourself before publishing rather than trusting a single automatic output.

Record what you find: the exact menu name, whether the translation is automatic, whether you can edit the translated lines, and which languages appear. Note the app version too. If the list changes later, your screenshots give you something concrete to compare against the coverage cited above.

If Nepali is not in the list, you can still caption a Reel in Nepali by writing and timing the captions yourself and adding them as text layers. This takes more effort than automatic captions, but it gives you control over wording and font. Test the rendering first, because a font that looks fine on a laptop can break on a phone.
